Background

I did my undergraduate degree in physics at UC Berkeley, with coursework in quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, electrodynamics, analytic mechanics, quantum computing and information, and advanced experimental physics. There I simulated Grover's search algorithm with and without the [[4,2,2]] stabilizer code in Qiskit, using algorithmic error tomography to identify dominant error channels, and in the advanced lab I extracted plasma transport quantities from probe measurements and ran spectroscopy, scattering, and optical-pumping experiments. I also spent a summer at IIT Delhi characterizing Mg-doped p-type GaN thin films by X-ray diffraction, Raman, and UV–Vis spectroscopy.

I am now a graduate researcher in Materials Science and Engineering at Duke, in the Finkelstein Quantum Materials and Devices Lab, working on low-dimensional quantum materials across theory, computation, and experiment. My Duke coursework covers solid-state physics and chemistry, advanced quantum mechanics, computational materials, materials synthesis and processing, nanoelectronics, and solid-state quantum devices.

In the lab, I fabricate hBN-encapsulated, graphite-gated van der Waals heterostructures by exfoliation and PC/PDMS dry transfer with deterministic alignment, and characterize them with atomic force microscopy and Raman spectroscopy. I am currently extending this workflow to incorporate WSe₂ into graphene-based stacks, for studies of WSe₂-induced spin–orbit proximity effects, superconducting proximity phenomena, and quantum Hall transport.

On the computational side, I use all-electron density functional theory (FHI-aims) to model these systems. I built and validated a 59-atom commensurate graphene/WSe₂ heterostructure and ran spin–orbit-coupled band-structure calculations of the graphene-derived Dirac bands within the WSe₂ gap, along with an interlayer-distance scan. This work connects to semiconductor physics and to how low-dimensional and quantum materials can extend conventional semiconductor technology, including quantum computing platforms.
