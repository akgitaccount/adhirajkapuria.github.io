Background & trajectory

I did my undergraduate degree in physics at UC Berkeley. My coursework there spanned quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, electrodynamics, analytic mechanics, quantum computing and information, and advanced experimental physics. I worked on both computational and experimental problems. In one project I simulated Grover's search algorithm with and without the [[4,2,2]] stabilizer code in Qiskit, comparing encoded and unencoded implementations under realistic noise and using algorithmic error tomography to identify the dominant error channels. I presented that work at the Berkeley Physics C191 exhibition. In the advanced lab, I extracted transport quantities like drift velocity, collision frequency, and electron temperature from probe measurements of a plasma, and ran laser, spectroscopy, scattering, and optical-pumping experiments using lock-in detection, model fitting, and uncertainty analysis.

I am now a graduate researcher in Materials Science and Engineering at Duke, in the Finkelstein Quantum Materials and Devices Lab, working on low-dimensional quantum materials across theory, computation, and experiment. My coursework at Duke covers solid-state physics and chemistry, advanced quantum mechanics, computational materials, materials synthesis and processing, nanoelectronics, and solid-state quantum devices.

In the lab, I fabricate hBN-encapsulated, graphite-gated van der Waals heterostructures. This involves exfoliating atomically thin crystals, identifying flakes optically, and assembling stacks by PC/PDMS dry transfer with deterministic alignment. I characterize the stacks with atomic force microscopy and Raman spectroscopy to estimate layer thickness, assess topography, and identify defects such as bubbles, wrinkles, and residues, and I work on optimizing exfoliation, annealing, and transfer to improve yield and interface quality. I am currently extending this workflow to incorporate WSe₂ into graphene-based stacks, for studies of WSe₂-induced spin–orbit proximity effects, superconducting proximity phenomena, and quantum Hall transport.

On the computational side, I use all-electron density functional theory (FHI-aims) to model these systems from first principles. I constructed and geometrically validated a 59-atom commensurate graphene/WSe₂ heterostructure and ran spin–orbit-coupled band-structure calculations of the graphene-derived Dirac bands within the WSe₂ gap. I also set up an interlayer-distance scan to study how the electronic structure depends on interface spacing.

This work connects to semiconductor physics, and to how low-dimensional and quantum materials can extend conventional semiconductor technology, including quantum computing platforms relevant to some of my fabrication work.
