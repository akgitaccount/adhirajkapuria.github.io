Research

Low-dimensional quantum materials

My research addresses low-dimensional quantum materials from three complementary directions — the theory that governs their electronic structure, the fabrication of the devices themselves, and first-principles computation of their properties. The unifying question is how the behavior of a material emerges from, and can be engineered through, its structure at the atomic scale.

Working across theory, computation, and experiment reflects a conviction that understanding a material means seeing it from all three sides: the theory sets what is worth building and calculating, computation predicts what an interface should do, and fabrication tests it. Much of this work connects to semiconductor physics and to quantum computing platforms, where these materials move from objects of study toward the basis for real devices.

Theory → Graphene, band topology, and proximity effects. Nano-fabrication → Van der Waals heterostructures and characterization. Computation → First-principles electronic structure.
